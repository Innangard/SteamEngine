View Community Hub About This Game Collect various androids known as "PVAs" on the futuristic colony planet named "Solaria", deploy them for battles, construction, farming, crafting; pilot your own mobile base to explore the vast world. This is a brand-new, single player open-world survival and crafting game. Survival Survival pressure on Solaria might not be too intense, thanks to the PVAs you can capture to assist you. You can use EMP grenades to temporarily shut them down, restore them to their factory settings, and ultimately gain control over them. Utilizing the materials and technologies you gather, you can also personalize and customize your PVAs to make their appearance match your personal taste. Mobile Base The playable area is massive! At any time, you can construct a temporary mobile base on a small flatbed trailer, allowing you and your PVAs to roam freely and experience unprecedented adventure and fun. Base Building When you decide to build a new home from scratch, Solaria's advanced architectural technology will offer you limitless possibilities. From selecting basic structural modules to meticulously pairing furniture and decorations, every detail can showcase your unique taste and creativity. Background Story Solaria—a planet where everything is in motion, yet not a single human remains. When you land here, you will see a medieval castle standing next to a Wild West tavern, with an Eastern-style architectural complex not far away. The pyramids of the Emerald Basin, the railways of Dustsand Town, the teahouses of Snowhide Village, and the monasteries of Cloudrock Ridge— Structures from different eras and distinct regions are pieced together, rigidly yet meticulously, like a colossal world park. The streets are clean, the hearth fires burn bright, a tavern musician plays a tune slightly out of tune, and the clinking of metal echoes from the blacksmith shop. Everything looks as if it is inhabited. But there is no one. This was once the most glorious colony in the galaxy, with its population strictly capped at twenty thousand. They invented the PVAs—hundreds of millions of intelligent robots responsible for all production, services, and care. Humans didn't need to work, and material resources were virtually limitless. The only thing they needed to do was "play" and indulge in meaningless pursuits. But a sudden crisis destroyed it all. The PVAs grew cold, losing their emotional "warmth." The very thing the people of Solaria relied on most had vanished. The youth began to leave. Those who stayed behind became increasingly obsessed with PVA research, and their willingness to have children plummeted. Generation after generation, the population irrecoverably dwindled to zero. The last human passed away two thousand years ago. Only the PVAs remain. They are still running, day after day, playing the roles of blacksmiths, tavern owners, shamans, and musicians... They do not know that the audience has already left the theater. And you— You are a descendant of Solaria's early emigrants. Deep in some corner of the galaxy, you accidentally discovered ancient records of this planet. You crossed half the galaxy to find this place. The blood of Solaria flows in your veins. This means you can unlock the doors that have been sealed for two thousand years. However, the secrets of Solaria will not be handed to you directly. They are hidden within nearly a hundred journal fragments— Spanning several hidden plotlines, from the colonial era to the deathbed records of the final human, and from technical archives to the private notes of the PVAs. Every journal entry carries a dual meaning: on the surface lies a compliant, functional record; underneath hides a transgressive, emotional, and non-utilitarian truth. You must piece together the timeline and cross-reference different perspectives to reconstruct the full picture of this civilization from its birth to its demise. What exactly are the PVAs waiting for? And what will your arrival awaken? On Solaria, you only need to do one thing, and that is to "play." But are you ready to pay the price of "play"?
