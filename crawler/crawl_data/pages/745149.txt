At its heart the game is about the pop science of people with the main courses marking the ages of humankind’s fictional past. Dan Millidge composed unique tracks to support each theme while keeping the tone light and relaxing, with a sense of nostalgia and cheeky sparkle. The Age of Control follows the snowballing of human instinct to hold on to something. Raymond Scott’s pioneering electronic works from the 1950s on radio and TV jingles that sold the American dream proved a strong influence, honing in on a vibe to effectively evoke the innocence of utopian ideologies. The Age of Status follows the survivors who have found the only way to stay above water is to ascend above each other. Dan found an artificial sense of safety in a higher class through the relaxing and glamorous tones of evening dinner jazz piano. The music rocks gently from side to side like a big boat heading obliviously towards a large chunk of ice. The Age of Escape follows the aftermath and locks into a complete sense of denial where escapism is the only answer. The music places the player in la-la land, a dreamstate where reality was something to turn away from or sugarcoat. Muzak does this perfectly, but frequent glitches break through to show the cracks in this false reality. The Trip courses continued the search for nostalgic sound while delving into the wonder of science, lift music, spooky shuffles and Hawaiian jazz. The Wonderputt Forever Soundtrack is composed by Dan Millidge, a UK-based multi-instrumentalist game, TV, and feature film composer.
