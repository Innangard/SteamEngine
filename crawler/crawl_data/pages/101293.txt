A delightfully paranormal point-and-click adventure… and deeply stupid whenever it needs to be. 1960. Navy Island, somewhere in the Caribbean. A quiet, tropical, humid island, a little too lush… in short, perfect for a vacation. Except you’re not on vacation. You’re Archibald Figg, an agent of MI-13 — the secret branch that handles the things the government prefers to file under “miscellaneous.” Your mission: figure out what’s happening on this island. Problem: everyone seems to be behaving normally… but absolutely nothing actually is. Explore. Talk. Snoop around. Try not to panic. Navy Island Confidential is a funny, strange, chill narrative adventure where the tropical vibe joyfully contrasts with suspicious apparitions, eccentric locals, and perpetual humidity. A narrative point-and-click adventure, modern yet faithful to the genre No reflex challenges, no stressful timers, no enemies chasing you because you clicked on the wrong pixel. Just you, your common sense (good luck), and an island full of oddities. Every location is designed like a mini theater scene: you observe, interact, ask unnecessary but extremely important questions, and gradually unearth a paranormal plot that really could have stayed buried… but no. This is a narrative adventure where you: • savor every line of dialogue, • follow the mysteries like a three-piece-suit detective, • let yourself drift into the atmosphere and its catchy music, • and laugh out loud at absurd situations that you seem to be the only one finding abnormal. A true point-and-click: fun, authentic, and frustration-free Navy Island Confidential embraces the spirit of the classics… but removes the parts that made players yell “WHY WOULD YOU DO THAT?!” at the screen. You know those impossible combinations like “sardine + diving suit to open a door”? Forget that. Here, the rule is simple: everything must be logical, smooth, and fun. No artificial puzzles, no padded-out playtime, no cryptic riddles invented at 3 a.m. Just pure enjoyment: • clear interactions, • coherent objectives, • natural progression, • and above all… situations absurd enough that complex puzzles aren’t even necessary. The adventure moves forward because you explore, not because you suffer. Shocking, right? Hilarious dialogues Archibald Figg has a knack for asking overly serious questions to people who are far too strange. The result: a festival of deadpan lines, absurd humor, polite sarcasm, and conversations that derail just the right amount. An indie game with no compromises and no microtransactions Developed by a slightly mad solo developer who’d rather do things right than do them just for money?
