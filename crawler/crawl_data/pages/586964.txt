Eight deadlines, each one steeper than the last. You have a slot machine and four spins per cycle to cover what you owe. Whatever is left over is yours to spend. It is also the only way you will ever be strong enough to survive the next deadline. That is the whole game. Build a machine, not a hand Every symbol you own sits in the reels. Every charm you equip bends the maths. 22 symbols — fruit, metal, wilds, and cursed ones that pay enormous and cost you later 40 charms — conditional multipliers, compound interest, RNG manipulation, and build enablers that make an entire archetype viable Buy, remove, upgrade — a crowded deck rarely lines up. Knowing what to cut is half the game Multipliers apply once per combination. Concentrate your deck, chain the right charms, and a single spin can pay a debt you could not have imagined ten minutes earlier. Seven machines, eight stakes The Classic — three reels, three rows, where you learn The Wide — four reels, lower values, longer lines The Held — hold a symbol between spins The Tall — three reels, five rows, verticals that run deep The Broken — one cell of the grid is dead, and it moves every cycle The Blind — the last reel stays covered. Pay to see it, or spin without knowing The Double — two grids, one budget of spins Each machine carries eight escalating stakes. Higher quotas, pricier shops, fewer spins, fewer charm slots — and at the top, a creditor who curses your deck and breaks the symbol you built everything around. Numbers that go very large Multipliers stack per combination. The ceiling is not a design decision, it is arithmetic — and reaching it is a build you construct on purpose, not a jackpot you wait for. No real money. Ever. Lucky Debt is a game about debt, not a gambling simulator. There are no purchases, no real currency, and nothing to win but the next cycle.
