The Mühldorf–Freilassing railway dates back to the 1890s, winding 65 km through the Bavarian countryside. The line is one of several in Germany yet to still be electrified, so services on the route are provided by diesel traction throughout. At Freilassing, the line joins the Rosenheim-Salzburg main line, immediately crossing the border into Austria before running the last few km into Salzburg. Both passenger and freight traffic traverse the line, although usually at no greater than an hourly frequency, but what the line doesn’t have in traffic it makes up for with unrestricted countryside views. Today, the line is served by DB, and their modern DB BR 245 diesel locomotive is well equipped for mixed-traffic use, making it the perfect loco for this wire-free railway. Stretching a total of 72 km, and featuring 13 detailed stations, you can discover this quaint cross-border line for yourself with RSSLO’s Salzburg - Mühldorf route.
